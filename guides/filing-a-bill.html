Filing a bill: how the pieces fit

You don't typeset your bill. You give the system two things — a few answers on the File or amend a bill form, and your legal text in one of your two Draft Google Docs — and it builds the formatted bill, numbers it, emails you the PDF, and posts it to the site. Here is which part of the finished bill comes from where.

1
Form — Short subject. A title of a few words, with no period at the end and at most 100 characters. It also appears in the bill tables, on agendas, and on the site.
2
Form — Digest. A plain-text description of existing law and what your bill changes. Several paragraphs are fine. Just the description — not the legal text, and not a copy of the header.
3
Form — Flags. Check what applies: Appropriations/fiscal, Local program, 2/3rds vote. Leave 2/3rds unchecked for a majority-vote bill. The two-thirds threshold applies only to the vote on the Senate floor; committees decide every bill by a majority of members present.
4
Your Draft doc — the legal text. Everything below the enacting clause comes from your Draft A or Draft B doc, exactly as you format it. Italic language prints blue (added to existing law); struck-through language prints red (removed). Plain text for brand-new sections. Never add color yourself.
Everything unshaded is added for you: the header, the bill number, the author line (from your registration, so sign in with the account you registered with), the digest's first line, the rule, and the enacting clause. Don't type any of it in your doc.

The steps, in order

  1. Write the legal text first, in one of your two Google Docs ("LegSim — SB Draft A" and "SB Draft B"). One bill per doc. Delete the instructions at the top. Stay in Editing mode, not Suggesting.
  2. Open the form from the site's Filing Cabinet → File or amend a bill, signed in with your registered Google account. Choose New bill.
  3. Fill in the fields: the short subject, the digest, the flags, and at least one topic.
  4. Pick the draft that holds this bill's text (Draft A or Draft B), confirm it's final, and submit.
  5. Check your email. Within a minute or two you get "Filed: SB-n" with the finished PDF attached. Read it. The site shows the bill about ten minutes later. No email means nothing was filed — almost always because you were signed into a different Google account.

Need to fix something? Correct your doc or your answers, then use the same form and choose Amendment. Pick your bill, leave every "Updated" field blank unless you're changing it, pick the same draft, and submit. The bill keeps its number.

Never choose "New bill" a second time for the same bill. That files a duplicate with a new number.

The three mistakes we see most

  • Submitting before the doc has your legal text — the bill prints with only the placeholder instructions.
  • Pasting the whole bill into the doc — the header and digest print twice.
  • Leaving the doc's instructions in — they print as part of your bill.

Your Draft docs are shared to your registered account; look for "LegSim — SB Draft" in Drive under Shared with me. Questions? Ask your instructor.
