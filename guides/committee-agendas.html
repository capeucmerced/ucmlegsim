Filing a committee agenda

Chairs don't typeset agendas. You open your committee's agenda form, give the meeting date and the bills in the order you will hear them, and the system builds the Senate-letterhead agenda, emails you the PDF, and posts it on your committee's tab of the Leadership & Committees page. Here is which part of the finished agenda comes from where.

Which form you open. There are five agenda forms in the Filing Cabinet: one per committee and one for the Senate Floor. The form sets the letterhead and the tab the agenda is posted on. A Floor agenda reads "Session of the Senate" and has no membership lines.
2
Form — Meeting Date. The date of the hearing. A committee has one agenda per date: filing the same date again makes a revision of that agenda, not a second one.
3
Form — Bills in file order, one per line. Just the bill number on each line, e.g. SB-12, in the order you will take them up. The author and short subject are looked up and printed for you. Up to 30 bills. A number that matches no filed bill prints the red "not found" note instead, so you can spot the typo at once.
Everything unshaded is automatic: the chair, vice chair, and members (from the Committee assignments & leadership form; if a name is wrong, that is where it gets fixed), the spelled-out date, the file name, and the revision notice. Agendas carry no time or room; announce those in class.

The steps, in order

  1. Wait for the referrals. Agenda only bills that have been referred to your committee; the Bills page's Committee column shows which ones those are. There is no draft mode: every submission is published, so don't file a practice agenda or one for a meeting that hasn't been scheduled.
  2. Open your committee's form from the Filing Cabinet → Leadership & chairs → File an agenda — [your committee], signed in with your registered Google account.
  3. Enter the meeting date.
  4. List the bills, one number per line, in the order you will hear them. Nothing else goes on a line.
  5. Submit and check your email. Within a minute or two you get "Agenda filed: Committee, date" with the PDF attached. Read it. The site shows it as the committee's Latest agenda about ten minutes later.

Need to change it? Submit the same form again with the same meeting date. That files Revision 2: the new PDF replaces the old one on the site, carries the red REVISED line with its revision number and time, and the earlier version is kept on file. A different date is a different meeting: a new agenda, not a revision.

A bill referred to you isn't yours? Referrals are leadership's call. Ask them to move it with the referrals form rather than leaving it off silently.

The mistakes we see most

  • Filing on another committee's form. The agenda prints under the wrong committee name and lands on the wrong tab.
  • Filing early, as a test or a placeholder. It is published immediately and the instructor has to remove it by hand.
  • Putting more than the number on a line. Only the first number counts: "SB-12 and SB-14" lists SB-12 alone, and "Item 2: SB-14" lists SB-2.
  • Picking the wrong date. That creates a separate agenda; refile with the right date and ask the instructor to remove the stray one.

Posted agendas are named by committee and date (for example lgl_10_20_26.pdf); earlier revisions stay available under "Earlier agendas". Questions? Ask your instructor.
