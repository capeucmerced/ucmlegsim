Recording votes

The committee secretary keeps the roll in the shared vote entry sheet: one row per roll call, one column per member. The sheet does the counting, the chair announces from it, and the website updates itself. The numbered badges below match the steps.

  1. Open the sheet and click your committee's tab. The link is on the website's Votes page; no sign-in needed. Keep the agenda open too (Leadership & Committees → your committee → Latest agenda) so the bills go in file order. The Floor tab works the same way for the Secretary of the Senate.
  2. Pick the bill from the Bill dropdown in the first empty row. The date fills itself — change it only for a vote from another day. A bill that isn't in the list hasn't been filed: tell the chair.
  3. Call the roll in column order: Aye, No, or Abstain from each member's dropdown. Absent means leave it blank (row 3, column H). A member who isn't on your tab hasn't registered — tell the instructor.
  4. Pick Pass or Fail in Result once the last vote is in. Pass needs more Ayes than half of Present — 3 of 5 in row 2. (Floor only: a bill whose digest says Vote: 2/3rds needs two-thirds.)
  5. Keep the roll tidy. Next bill, next row. If a vote you set up doesn't happen, delete its row — a half-filled row is a wrong record. A bill voted on again later gets a new row; the newest one counts. The website picks everything up on its own within about an hour.

Don'ts

  • Never type a vote. A typed Aye with a stray space once went uncounted. If a cell has no dropdown, stop and tell the instructor. Wrong pick? Just pick again.
  • Don't touch the header row or the Ayes and Present columns — they warn you if you click in; choose Cancel.
  • Don't pick Pass or Fail before the roll call is finished — and don't leave it blank afterwards, or the website shows the bill as still undecided.
  • Don't record in another committee's tab — the mistake we see most.
